The average US small business owner spends 16+ hours per week on administrative tasks that could be automated. That's two full workdays. Automation tools give that time back — here are the best ones available in 2026.

## What Business Tasks Can Be Automated?

Almost more than you'd think:

- Moving data between apps (CRM → spreadsheet → email)
- Sending follow-up emails after form submissions
- Creating invoices when a project is marked complete
- Posting to social media on a schedule
- Syncing inventory across sales channels
- Notifying your team when a new lead arrives

---

## 1. Zapier — Best All-Around Automation Platform

**Pricing:** Free (limited) / $19.99/month (Starter) / $49/month (Professional)

Zapier connects 6,000+ apps with point-and-click automation — no coding needed. It's the most widely used automation tool among US small businesses, and for good reason: setup takes minutes and it just works.

**Best for:** Any business connecting common SaaS apps like Gmail, Slack, Salesforce, QuickBooks, Shopify

**Pros:**
- Largest app library (6,000+ integrations)
- No code required — genuinely beginner-friendly
- Excellent documentation and templates
- Reliable uptime

**Cons:**
- Gets expensive as automation volume grows
- Free plan is very limited (100 tasks/month)
- Complex workflows require higher-tier plans

---

## 2. Make (formerly Integromat) — Best for Complex Workflows

**Pricing:** Free (1,000 ops/month) / $9/month (Core) / $16/month (Pro)

Make is Zapier's most powerful competitor, with a visual canvas that lets you build multi-step, branching automation flows. It's more complex to learn but dramatically more affordable for high-volume automation.

**Best for:** Tech-savvy users, agencies, businesses with high automation volume

**Pros:**
- Visual workflow builder is genuinely impressive
- Much cheaper than Zapier at scale
- Handles complex, conditional logic well
- Excellent error handling

**Cons:**
- Steeper learning curve than Zapier
- Smaller app library (still 1,000+ integrations)

---

## 3. n8n — Best Free/Self-Hosted Option

**Pricing:** Free (self-hosted) / $20/month (Cloud Starter)

n8n is the automation tool that tech-forward US businesses are quietly switching to. The self-hosted version is completely free with unlimited automation runs. The cloud version is affordable and requires no server management.

**Best for:** Businesses wanting zero usage limits, developers, privacy-conscious businesses

**Pros:**
- Self-hosted = completely free, unlimited runs
- Open source with huge community
- Advanced features: code nodes, custom API calls, AI integration
- Growing fast with frequent updates

**Cons:**
- Self-hosting requires technical setup
- Smaller template library than Zapier/Make

---

## 4. Airtable Automations — Best If You're Already on Airtable

**Pricing:** Included with Airtable (Free–$45/user/month)

If your business already uses Airtable as a database, its built-in automation feature is underused gold. Trigger emails, Slack messages, record updates, and external webhooks directly from your Airtable base without switching tools.

**Best for:** Teams using Airtable for project management, CRM, or inventory

**Pros:**
- No extra subscription needed
- Tightly integrated with your data
- Easy to set up for non-technical users

**Cons:**
- Only useful if you're already in Airtable
- Limited compared to dedicated automation platforms

---

## 5. Monday.com Automations — Best for Project-Based Businesses

**Pricing:** Included with Monday.com ($9–$19/user/month)

Monday.com's built-in automations handle the repetitive parts of project management: assigning tasks, sending deadline reminders, notifying clients, and updating statuses based on triggers. Construction firms, agencies, and event companies use this heavily.

**Best for:** Service businesses, agencies, project-based teams

**Pros:**
- No extra cost — included with Monday subscription
- Simple automation builder
- Works across all boards and projects

**Cons:**
- Limited to Monday.com's ecosystem
- Not suitable for cross-app automation

## Start Small, Then Scale

The biggest mistake US small business owners make with automation is trying to automate everything at once. Start with one painful, repetitive task — like manually copying form submissions into your CRM — and automate just that. Once you see the time savings, you'll naturally find the next thing to automate.

Most small businesses find their first 3–5 automations cover 80% of the repetitive work. That's when the ROI becomes undeniable.
