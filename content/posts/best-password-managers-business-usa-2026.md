The 2024 Verizon Data Breach Investigations Report found that 81% of hacking-related breaches in the US involved stolen or weak passwords. For US small businesses, a single compromised password can expose customer data, financial accounts, and company systems — triggering lawsuits, regulatory fines, and permanent reputation damage.

A business password manager solves this at the root. Every employee gets a unique, complex password for every account, stored securely, shared safely within teams, and revoked instantly when someone leaves. Here are the best options for US businesses in 2026.

---

## Why Individual Password Managers Are Not Enough for Business

Consumer password managers like the free tier of LastPass protect one person. Business password managers do much more:

- **Shared vaults** — Share team passwords securely without emailing them
- **Admin controls** — See which employees have access to which accounts
- **Offboarding** — Remove departing employees' access to all company accounts instantly
- **Security audit** — Identify weak, reused, or compromised passwords across the team
- **SSO integration** — Connect with Okta, Azure AD, and Google Workspace

## Password Security Rules Every US Business Should Enforce

**Minimum 16 characters.** Password managers generate these automatically — length is the most important security factor.

**Unique password for every account.** Never reuse passwords. If one account is breached, others stay safe.

**Enable multi-factor authentication.** Combine your password manager with an authenticator app like Google Authenticator or Duo. Even if a password is stolen, MFA blocks the attacker.

**Audit quarterly.** Run a password health report every quarter. Remove old accounts, update weak passwords, and check for breaches.

**Revoke access immediately on departure.** The day an employee leaves, remove their access from the password manager. This should be part of your standard offboarding checklist.
